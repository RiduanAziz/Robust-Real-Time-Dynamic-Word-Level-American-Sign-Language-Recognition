    SignFlow: Project Defense, Oral Exam & Viva Voce Guide¶

    Project: Robust Real-Time Dynamic Word-Level American Sign Language Recognition
    Candidate / Intern: Riduan Aziz
    Target Audience: Academic Examination Committee, Project Evaluation Mentors, Technical Panel
    Primary Artifact: docs/INTERNSHIP_FINAL_PROJECT_REPORT.md

    1. The 2-Minute Executive Pitch (Opening Statement)¶

    "Distinguished committee and mentors, dynamic word-level sign language recognition represents one of the most demanding frontiers in human-computer interaction. While state-of-the-art vision models typically rely on heavy 3D-CNNs that take 500 milliseconds per sample and cannot run in real time on edge hardware, standard landmark models break down when signers occlude their hands or perform one-handed gestures.

    In this internship, I developed SignFlow—an end-to-end, high-performance recognition ecosystem. My work delivers three key breakthroughs: 1. First, I audited the 2,038-sequence WLASL-100 benchmark and uncovered critical real-world modality asymmetries: non-dominant hands are absent in two-thirds of signing frames, proving that naive zero-imputation creates artificial high-velocity motion noise. 2. Second, to solve this, I designed and adapted an ICLR 2025 Uni-Sign Prior-Guided Fusion network with score-aware reliability gating, and combined it with regularized deep recurrent models into a 3-Way Probability Ensemble. Tuned strictly on validation data with zero test leakage, our ensemble sets a new benchmark: 76.65% Top-1 Accuracy (+2.33% over baseline), 91.44% Top-5 Accuracy, and reduces calibration error by 47.6%. 3. Third, rather than stopping at an offline notebook, I built and verified a full-stack production workstation using FastAPI WebSockets and a React 18 glassmorphic frontend that supports live multi-camera input and dynamic model switching with an end-to-end latency of just 31.7 milliseconds.

    Every result in this project is reproducible via deterministic scripts and backed by 19 automated regression tests. I am excited to walk you through the architecture, the empirical discoveries, and a live demonstration."

    3. High-Stakes Examiner Questions & Bulletproof Answers¶

    Q1: "Why did you use MediaPipe holistic landmarks instead of an end-to-end video model like I3D or SlowFast?"¶

    • Examiner's Intent: Testing your understanding of the latency-accuracy-privacy trade-off in assistive technology.
    • Your Answer:

      "End-to-end 3D-CNNs like I3D achieve ~65.89% Top-1 on WLASL-100 (Li et al., 2020), but require approximately 500 milliseconds per sample on a desktop GPU and tens of gigabytes of VRAM. In an assistive system, deaf users require immediate real-time feedback (<50 ms glass-to-glass latency). Furthermore, raw RGB video introduces substantial user privacy concerns in domestic or workplace settings. By decoupling perception via client-side MediaPipe extraction, we stream 553 geometric coordinates instead of high-bandwidth video, preserving privacy and shrinking end-to-end inference latency to ~31.7 milliseconds (over 15x faster than I3D) while achieving a superior 76.65% Top-1 accuracy."

    Q2: "How did you ensure there was zero data leakage when finding the ensemble weights?"¶

    • Examiner's Intent: Checking scientific integrity and hyperparameter overfitting.
    • Your Answer:

      "We strictly followed an isolated partition protocol. The ensemble weights \((w_1, w_2, w_3)\) were optimized via an exhaustive 3-simplex grid search exclusively on the 337 validation samples (data/manifests/validation_manifest.json). The optimal configuration—\((0.25, 0.10, 0.65)\)—was selected based solely on validation Top-1 accuracy (78.34%). Only after these weights were completely locked was the ensemble evaluated in a single forward pass on the 257 held-out test samples. The test set was never accessed during hyperparameter tuning, preventing any data snooping or leakage."

    Q3: "Solo Uni-Sign achieved 70.43% Top-1, which is lower than the 74.32% baseline. Why include it in the ensemble?"¶

    • Examiner's Intent: Testing whether you understand ensemble theory and complementary inductive biases.
    • Your Answer:

      "In ensembling, the goal is not to combine models with identical error profiles, but to combine models with orthogonal inductive biases. While the baseline BiGRU models temporal sequential transitions, Uni-Sign PGF explicitly models spatial cross-attention between hands and body anchors. Most importantly, Uni-Sign possesses exceptional probability calibration: its Expected Calibration Error (ECE) is 0.0561, compared to 0.1598 for the baseline. When blended with the baseline and deep BiGRU, Uni-Sign dampens overconfident incorrect predictions. This complementary spatial-temporal bias is why the ensemble jumped to 76.65% Top-1 and reduced overall ECE by 47.6%."

    Q4: "What is the significance of your EDA findings on hand detection rates?"¶

    • Examiner's Intent: Verifying whether you actually analyzed the dataset before training models.
    • Your Answer:

      "Our empirical audit across the 2,038 sequences revealed that body pose (99.4%) and face mesh (97.3%) are almost universally visible, but the right hand is present in only 63.6% of frames, and the left hand in only 32.5%. In sign language, many signs are unilateral (one-handed). Naive concatenation imputes missing hands with \((0, 0, 0)\), creating artificial high-velocity motion vectors when hands appear or disappear. This discovery directly inspired our reliability gating mechanism, where hand attention maps are scaled by their empirical presence score \(r_m\), mathematically zeroing out artificial gradient noise."

    Q5: "What is the difference between Protocol A and Protocol B, and why does it matter?"¶

    • Examiner's Intent: Testing your awareness of academic benchmark flaws and real-world generalization.
    • Your Answer:

      "Protocol A is the official WLASL benchmark split. However, our audit revealed that 104 out of 105 signers (99.0%) in the test set also appear in the training set. While this allows models to learn signer-specific biometric habits, it does not evaluate how the model handles unseen people. To address this, we engineered Protocol B (data/manifests/signer_disjoint/), which strictly partitions signers with zero overlap. Protocol A proves that our architecture outperforms published literature under standard conditions (76.65% vs 74.32%), while Protocol B provides an honest, rigorous foundation for evaluating true out-of-distribution generalizability."

    Q6: "How does the system perform when deployed to a real webcam in a web browser?"¶

    • Examiner's Intent: Assessing full-stack deployment, systems architecture, and practical engineering.
    • Your Answer:

      "We built an end-to-end full-stack application. In the frontend (React 18 / TypeScript), MediaPipe's WebAssembly pipeline processes camera frames client-side and transmits normalized landmarks over a persistent WebSocket connection to an asynchronous FastAPI backend. The backend maintains a rolling FIFO window of 64 frames. Complete round-trip processing takes ~31.7 ms, allowing us to run at 30+ frames per second. Furthermore, our frontend includes a dynamic camera selector allowing users to switch video inputs on the fly, and a model selector that hot-switches inference architectures without server restarts."

    4. Live Demonstration Script (Step-by-Step Playbook)¶

    Follow this 5-step walkthrough during your live project presentation:

    Step 1: System Health & Architecture Overview (1 min)
  - Open browser at http://localhost:5173/
  - Point out the dark AI-workstation interface, WebSocket status badge (green: Connected), and GPU/CPU latency telemetry.
  - Explain that the backend is currently running FastAPI on port 8000.

Step 2: Model Architecture Switching (1.5 min)
  - Direct the committee's attention to the Model Selector dropdown in the top navigation bar.
  - Show the 4 options:
      1. 3-Way Calibrated Ensemble (Default Winning Model)
      2. Uni-Sign Prior-Guided Fusion
      3. Deep BiGRU (Max Accuracy)
      4. Baseline BiGRU
  - Select "Uni-Sign Prior-Guided Fusion". Show the instantaneous model hot-switch without connection interruption.

Step 3: Multi-Camera Hardware Switching (1 min)
  - Show the Camera Device dropdown in the top header and camera toolbar.
  - Switch from the default laptop camera to a secondary external USB webcam / virtual camera.
  - Demonstrate that the video stream renegotiates and MediaPipe tracking re-locks automatically.

Step 4: Live Recognition & Confidence Telemetry (2 min)
  - Perform dynamic signs (e.g., "computer", "book", "chair", "yes", "hot").
  - Show the top-5 prediction bar chart updating in real time.
  - Point out the calibrated confidence score and latency counter (~31 ms).
  - Demonstrate the Text-to-Speech (TTS) audio feedback speaking the predicted sign.

Step 5: Automated Testing & Reproducibility Proof (1 min)
  - Switch to terminal and run:
      pytest tests/test_accuracy_optimization.py tests/test_features_contract.py -q
  - Show all 19 tests passing instantly, proving deterministic correctness and zero regression.

    6. Examiner Trap Avoidance Checklist¶

    • [x] Never claim Protocol A is signer-independent: Be upfront that Protocol A has 99% signer overlap. Highlight that you uncovered this and engineered Protocol B to address it.
    • [x] Never hide the solo Uni-Sign Top-1 score (70.43%): Emphasize that Uni-Sign's value lies in its 0.0561 ECE calibration and hand-to-pose spatial attention, which enables the ensemble to beat all solo models.
    • [x] Never claim 3-way ensemble is heavy: The ensemble performs three forward passes on tiny recurrent/convolutional models totaling <15 MB of parameters, executing in ~11.9 ms on CPU.
    • [x] Always mention the test set was locked: Explicitly state that weights were chosen on the 337 validation samples, and the test split was evaluated only once.
